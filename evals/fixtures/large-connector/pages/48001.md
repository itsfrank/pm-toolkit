# Warehouse tiered storage design

Status: **Proposed**, in design review. Not approved for build. Author: Mei Arakawa (Infrastructure). Reviewers: Tomas Lindqvist, Rafael Osei, Aisha Karim (for audit data).

## Summary
Kestrel Warehouse keeps every event on local NVMe for the full retention window (3, 6 or 13 months by plan). Storage is now the largest line in the Warehouse bill and grows roughly with event volume. This page proposes splitting stored events into three tiers: **hot** (NVMe, fast queries), **warm** (columnar Parquet on object storage) and **cold** (archive, restore on request). Query semantics do not change; only latency for older ranges does.

*This is a design document. Numbers marked (est.) come from the March-September 2026 usage sample and have not been validated on production tenants.*

## Background
- Warehouse writers batch events into 256 MB segments, sorted by tenant and event time.
- Insights v2 queries scan segments directly; there is no separate OLAP layer.
- Today 100% of segments sit on `wh-hot-*` nodes until retention expires, then are deleted by the janitor job.
- In the usage sample, 91% of queries touch only the last 30 days of data (est.). Queries over more than 90 days are about 2% of volume but 24% of scanned bytes.

## Goals and non-goals
**Goals**
- Reduce Warehouse storage cost without changing retention promises.
- Keep p95 dashboard query latency for the last 21 days unchanged.
- Make tier placement per tenant policy, not a global switch.

**Non-goals**
- Changing plan retention (13 / 6 / 3 months).
- Changing the EU region design; tiering in eu-central-1 is a follow-up to [Project Fjord].
- Customer-visible tier controls in the UI in the first release.

## Proposed design
### Tier definitions
| Tier | Storage | Age window | Query path | Expected p95 (est.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hot | Local NVMe, i4i nodes | 0-21 days | Direct segment scan | 1.1 s dashboard load (unchanged) |
| Warm | Parquet on S3 Standard-IA | 22-180 days | Warm reader fleet, range-pruned | 4.8 s |
| Cold | S3 Glacier Instant Retrieval | 181 days to retention end | Restore job, then warm reader | 15 min restore, then warm latencies |

The **cold tier is Enterprise only**: Growth (6 months) and Starter (3 months) tenants never reach 181 days, so only the 13-month Enterprise plan has cold data.

### Segment lifecycle
1. Writer seals a segment and uploads it to the hot node.
2. A *tierer* job runs hourly and picks segments past the hot window for each tenant class.
3. The segment is rewritten as Parquet with a zstd level 6 codec, verified by row count and checksum, then registered in the manifest as `warm`.
4. The hot copy is deleted only after 24 hours of successful reads from the warm copy (shadow reads).
5. After day 180 the same flow moves the object to the cold storage class.

### Manifest change
```sql
ALTER TABLE segment_manifest
  ADD COLUMN tier TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'hot',
  ADD COLUMN tiered_at TIMESTAMP NULL,
  ADD COLUMN warm_uri TEXT NULL;

CREATE INDEX idx_manifest_tier_age
  ON segment_manifest (tier, sealed_at)
  WHERE tier <> 'cold';
```

### Policy file
```yaml
# tiering-policy.yaml (per tenant class, defaults)
hot_days: 21
warm_days: 180
cold_enabled: false     # true only for plan=enterprise
shadow_read_hours: 24
max_restore_concurrency: 4
```

## Rollout plan
- Phase 0: shadow tiering for 3 internal tenants, no deletes.
- Phase 1: Starter and Growth tenants, warm tier only.
- Phase 2: Enterprise tenants, cold tier on.
- **Proposed cutover date: 2027-03-15** (Phase 1 start). Not approved; depends on warm reader fleet load tests.

## Risks
| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm reader slower than estimate | Medium | Insights v2 dashboards over 30-90 days feel slow | Reader cache sized at 2x; per-tenant override back to hot |
| Parquet rewrite loses precision on float properties | Low | Wrong sums | Row-level checksum and property-type roundtrip test |
| Restore storms after an audit request | Low | Cold restores throttled | Concurrency cap of 4, queue visible in Lookout |
| S3 request cost higher than modelled | Medium | Saving shrinks | Segment size raised to 512 MB for warm objects |

## Appendix B: FAQ
**Will customers see a difference?** Only for ranges older than 21 days, where dashboards may take a few seconds longer. *No customer communication is planned until the proposal is approved.*

**What happens to deleted events (privacy requests)?** Deletion requests rewrite the affected Parquet object and tombstone the manifest row. Cold objects are restored, rewritten, and re-archived; this takes up to 24 hours.

**Can a tenant opt out?** Yes, through an override in `tiering-policy.yaml`, at the cost of staying on the hot fleet bill.

**Why not use a separate OLAP engine for old data?** Evaluated in August; operating cost and a second query dialect outweighed the saving. See the comparison notes in the INFRA space.

## Appendix D: glossary
- **Segment**: sealed, immutable batch of events for one tenant.
- **Tierer**: background job that moves segments between tiers.
- **Shadow read**: reading the warm copy in parallel with the hot one to compare results before deletion.
- **Janitor**: job that deletes expired segments.
